# Episode 01: ICT Mentorship 2022 Introduction

**Source URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kt6V4ai60fI

## Summary of Episode
This video serves as the official introduction and orientation for the 2022 ICT Mentorship on YouTube. Michael J. Huddleston (ICT) explains that this series is designed to teach a stripped-down, simplified trading model to his daughter, which he is opening up to the public. He addresses critics of his previous teaching methods by showing a live, funded TD Ameritrade (thinkorswim) account (balance of ~$23,694.11) with real-money trade executions. He introduces the goal of the mentorship: to consistently find and extract 25 index handles per week using E-mini S&P 500 (ES) or Micro E-mini (MES) futures. He lays down the psychological expectations of trading, outlines the schedule for upcoming lessons (Tuesdays and Thursdays), and demonstrates a simplified trade setup utilizing discount/premium arrays and liquidity draws on a 2-minute ES chart.

## Concrete Trading Rules

*   **RULE:** The baseline weekly objective for a consistently profitable trader in this model is to target 25 index handles per week on the E-mini S&P 500.
    *   **CATEGORY:** Take profit / General principle.
    *   **NUMBERS:** 25 handles per week.
    *   **SOURCE:** 25:17 (Said).
    *   **CLARITY:** CLEAR.

*   **RULE:** If a trader does not possess the margin requirement (approximately $12,000–$13,000) to trade one full contract of the E-mini S&P 500 (ES), they must utilize the Micro E-mini S&P 500 (MES) contract, which scales down the risk and reward to $5 per handle instead of $50 per handle.
    *   **CATEGORY:** Risk/position size.
    *   **NUMBERS:** ES margin: ~$12,000–$13,000; ES point value: $50/handle; MES point value: $5/handle.
    *   **SOURCE:** 25:26 (Said).
    *   **CLARITY:** CLEAR.

*   **RULE:** High precision is not required for trade entries; instead, the primary precision focus must be directed toward identifying correct directional bias and the specific liquidity pool (draw on liquidity) price is reaching for.
    *   **CATEGORY:** Entry / General principle.
    *   **NUMBERS:** Not stated.
    *   **SOURCE:** 28:28 (Said & Showed on chart).
    *   **CLARITY:** CLEAR.

## Conditions that Cancel a Setup / Filters (What NOT to do)
*   **Over-trading or Over-leveraging:** Michael warns that achieving immediate profitable results on a live account often causes traders to experience psychological euphoria, leading them to chase trade setups. Over-trading or over-leveraging immediately cancels the systematic benefits of the mentorship rules (21:30 - 22:15).
*   **Unregulated Brokers/Platforms:** Utilizing untrustworthy, un-regulated offshore forex brokers or platforms (like MT4/MT5) that allow manual edit controls of historical trade results is highly discouraged (18:40 - 20:00).

## Gold (XAUUSD) vs. Forex Pairs vs. Indices Differences
*   **CFD Restrictions for US Citizens:** Michael points out that US-based traders are legally restricted from trading CFDs (e.g., US500) due to regulatory guidelines. Consequently, US-based traders must trade indices via CME-regulated Futures exchanges (such as ES or MES), whereas international traders have the flexibility to utilize CFDs (26:10 - 26:40).
*   **Forex Applicability:** He notes that while this specific 2022 mentorship model will be shown primarily on Index Futures, the structural logic of premium/discount arrays and liquidity draws applies identically to Forex pairs (25:50).

## Ambiguities and Open Questions
*   **OTE (Optimal Trade Entry) Definition:** Michael outlines the OTE zone visually using a red rectangle on the 2-minute ES chart but does not define the exact Fibonacci retracement measurements or mathematical thresholds required to plot the zone. This is left to be unpacked in future episodes.
*   **Stop Loss Rules:** While stop-loss placements are alluded to during the chart walkthroughs, he does not declare exact tick/point constraints or dynamic rules for structural invalidation in this introductory episode.
