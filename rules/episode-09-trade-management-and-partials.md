## Summary of Episode
This episode focuses on the **Power of 3 (PO3)** concept—comprising **Accumulation, Manipulation, and Distribution (AMD)**—and outlines high-probability trading opportunities in the **New York PM session**. Michael J. Huddleston ("ICT") details how daily candlestick profiles translate into intraday price action, using New York local time anchors (specifically 8:30 AM as the daily opening reference). He introduces a critical filter for Index Futures: when a massive overnight run occurs during the London session, traders should completely avoid the New York AM session to evade market chop and instead look for high-probability setups during the New York PM session (after 1:00 PM, specifically inside the 2:00 PM hour) once morning or lunch session liquidity has been swept.

## Conditions that Cancel a Setup / Filters (What NOT to do)
1. **Large Overnight Range Run:** Do NOT trade the New York Morning (AM) session if a large, sweeping range expansion/price run occurs overnight between 12:00 AM and 5:00 AM New York Time. This signals a high probability of a consolidative, choppy AM session.
2. **Chasing Price:** Do NOT buy when price has already expanded aggressively without first tracing back to a discount zone, order block, or Fair Value Gap.
3. **Wick Violation Misconception:** Price trading through a Fair Value Gap with wicks does NOT invalidate the setup, provided the candle bodies respect the FVG parameters.

## Ambiguities and Open Questions
1. **Quantifying "Large Overnight Run":** The term "large overnight price run" is subjective. To automate this rule, a programmer must define a specific threshold (e.g., overnight range exceeding a certain percentage of the 20-period ATR, or a set point value).
2. **Wick vs. Body Violations:** While he mentions that candle bodies must respect the FVG boundaries, he does not state an exact percentage or time-threshold of body closure outside of the FVG that formally invalidates the level.
