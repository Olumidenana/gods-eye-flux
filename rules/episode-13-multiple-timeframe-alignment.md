## Summary of Episode
In this video, titled *"Market Structure For Precision Technicians: Advanced Price Action Theory – In Action"*, Michael J. Huddleston demonstrates how the market structure principles taught in Episode 12 translate into actual trade executions. Using the Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 futures (`MNQ`) on February 25, 2022, he walks through the top-down narrative from the Daily, 1-Hour, 15-Minute, 5-Minute, down to the 1-Minute and sub-minute charts.

He explains:
- Institutional order flow rules: In a bullish market structure, down-close candles act as bullish order blocks (support) and should not be violated; price should respect Fair Value Gaps (FVGs) and seek liquidity above old highs (Buy-side Liquidity).
- The 9:30 AM New York Equities Open "Judas Swing" trap lower into a discount array (OB/FVG) before expanding upward toward liquidity pools.
- Proper pyramiding/scaling logic: Building the largest base position first at the lowest price level and adding smaller positions as price rallies (never inverted pyramiding).
- Trailing stop-loss mechanics based on protected institutional order flow (moving stops below subsequent down-close candles once they are confirmed as support).

## Conditions that Cancel a Setup / Filters (What NOT to do)

1. **Do not sell short into a liquidity draw:** Do not enter short positions solely because the market looks "overbought" or hits a resistance level if the higher-timeframe algorithmic structure has uncleared buy-side liquidity above relative equal highs.
2. **Do not trade if live market uncertainty creates anxiety:** If external geopolitical shocks or high volatility create severe emotional distress (or if monthly performance targets have already been achieved), do not trade live funds; switch to a demo account.
3. **Do not invert pyramiding:** Never scale into a trade with larger position sizes than the initial entry (e.g., entering 1 contract first, then adding 2, then adding 3). The base order must always be the largest to maintain equity cushion and prevent the average entry price from advancing too close to the current price.
4. **Do not chase the open at 9:30 AM:** Do not buy high right before or immediately at 9:30 AM NY time; wait for the opening Judas swing to retrace into discount support.
5. **Do not trail stops prematurely inside an active order block:** Do not place trailing stops inside the active body or wick of a down-close candle before that candle has been cleared to the upside, as price frequently digs into down-close candle bodies to re-accumulate orders before continuing higher.

## Ambiguities and Open Questions

1. **Exact pip/tick distance for stop buffer:** When placing stops "below the low of the down-close candle," ICT does not specify whether a fixed buffer (e.g., 2–5 ticks) should be added to account for bid/ask spread on futures.
2. **Quantitative definition of "shallow retest" near targets:** ICT states that when price is very close to the profit objective, the algorithm will not allow price to retrace deep into an order block and will only make a small shallow dip. An algorithmic rule requires an exact mathematical parameter (e.g., target within X ticks or within 10–20% of the dealing range) to trigger this reduced retracement expectation.
3. **Automated multi-contract scaling rule:** While ICT shows a 3-2-1 contract pyramiding sequence in this example, he does not provide a fixed formula for determining these contract proportions relative to arbitrary account balances or variable volatility environments.
