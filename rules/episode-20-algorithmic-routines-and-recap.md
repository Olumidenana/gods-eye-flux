## Summary of Episode
In this episode, Michael J. Huddleston demonstrates how the 2022 mentorship model applies to the London session in Forex, focusing on EUR/USD and the US Dollar Index (DXY). He details the intermarket relationship where DXY acts as a barometer/counter-asset to EUR/USD. Using his forecast from Episode 19, he shows how DXY reached an institutional support level (99.95–99.92) during the London Open Killzone (02:00–05:00 NY time) to form the low of the day, creating a corresponding false breakout (Turtle Soup) and high of the day in EUR/USD above a previous week's high. He then steps down to a 2-minute chart on EUR/USD to show the precise market structure shift, energetic displacement, Fair Value Gap (FVG) entry, stop loss placement, drawdown tolerance, and multi-tiered profit targets.

## Conditions that Cancel a Setup / Filters (What NOT to do)

- **Do NOT enter on a limit order inside a Fair Value Gap before displacement occurs:** An order cannot be placed when price is still hovering near the FVG; price must display energetic displacement away from the gap and break a key short-term swing low first.
- **Do NOT enter without time-of-day alignment:** High-probability session reversals must occur within the specific killzone window (02:00–05:00 NY time for London Open).
- **Do NOT chase a trade:** If price displaces and trades down without immediately hitting the entry level, do not chase it with market orders; wait patiently within the remaining killzone window for the retracement to reach the limit order price.
- **Do NOT trade fixed dollar risks:** Never risk a static dollar figure (e.g., "$1,000 per trade") regardless of account size; position sizing must always derive from percentage-based equity risk.

## Ambiguities and Open Questions

1. **Exact Stop Loss Buffer:** Michael sets the stop loss at 1.09365 when the swing high is 1.09363 (a 0.2 pip / 2 pipettes buffer), while stating it should just be "above the high." In automated systems, should a standardized buffer (e.g., 1–2 pips plus spread) be added above the swing high to avoid spread-hunting on retail broker feeds?
2. **First Minor Low vs. Secondary Swing Low for MSS:** Michael bypassed the first minor low because candle 3 closed near its low without displacing away. The programmatic criteria to differentiate an invalid minor low from a valid swing low requires clear rules (e.g., minimum pip displacement or number of candles closing below the level).
3. **Limit Order Offset:** Michael mentions entering at 1.09244 (the exact candle 3 high), but notes that entering "maybe one pipette, maybe a half a pip or maybe a full pip above this candle's high" is acceptable. A coded algorithm requires a definitive single price point (e.g., strictly at candle 3's high).
