# Episode 32: 2022 ICT Mentorship Episode 32

**Source URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hzoMJQlEv5M

## Summary of Episode
In this episode, Michael J. Huddleston reviews market price action on the E-mini S&P 500 futures contract (ES June 2022) following an "outside day" on the daily chart. He explains how to identify consolidation/choppy days in advance (when the market approaches an old key high or low but stops just short of running it, e.g., missing an old daily low by a single handle). He warns against trading within the middle of the range on such days due to low 50/50 probability, choppy price action, and whipsawing stops. 

He demonstrates how algorithmically driven consolidation days resolve into high-probability setups during the final hour of equity trading (3:00 PM to 4:00 PM New York local time), where smart money engineers a run outside the established daily range into liquidity (buy stops above relative equal highs) to fill on-close orders before sharply Mean-Reverting back to the 50% midpoint of the daily range. He shows live trade executions entered at 3:09 PM NY time, taking partial profits at the midpoint of the daily range.

## Concrete Trading Rules

- **RULE:** If the previous day is an outside down-close day that falls short of taking out an old daily low (stops short by approximately 1 handle), expect the current day to be a choppy consolidation day.

  - **SOURCE:** 01:17–02:10, 03:22–03:40 (SAID and SHOWED).
  - **CLARITY:** Clear.

- **RULE:** When price is trading near the middle of an established consolidation range, the directional probability drops to 50/50; traders should not seek directional continuation setups within this zone.
  - **CATEGORY:** general principle
  - **NUMBERS:** 50/50 probability; 50% (midpoint) of range.
  - **SOURCE:** 04:38–05:05 (SAID and SHOWED).
  - **CLARITY:** Clear.

- **RULE:** On consolidation days, wait until the 3:00 PM to 4:00 PM NY local time window (equity close / market-on-close window) for the algorithm to engineer a false breakout outside the daily range to seek liquidity before returning to the range midpoint.
  - **CATEGORY:** session/killzone
  - **NUMBERS:** 3:00 PM to 4:00 PM New York local time.
  - **SOURCE:** 01:00–01:05, 06:33–06:58, 07:54–08:03, 11:26–11:45 (SAID and SHOWED).
  - **CLARITY:** Clear.

- **RULE:** When price sweeps buy stops above relative equal highs between 3:00 PM and 4:00 PM NY local time on a consolidation day, wait for a lower timeframe (1-minute) swing low breakdown with a Fair Value Gap (FVG), then enter short when price trades back up into that FVG.
  - **CATEGORY:** entry
  - **NUMBERS:** 1-minute chart, 3:00 PM to 4:00 PM NY local time.
  - **SOURCE:** 08:09–08:52, 09:04–09:09 (SAID and SHOWED).
  - **CLARITY:** Clear.

- **RULE:** When entering short on a 3:00 PM to 4:00 PM liquidity run on a consolidation day, place the initial stop loss above the newly formed swing high that swept the liquidity.
  - **CATEGORY:** stop loss
  - **NUMBERS:** Above the peak high of the 3:00–4:00 PM run (shown at 3945.00 vs entry at 3940.75; 4.25 handles risk).
  - **SOURCE:** 09:22–09:30, 10:04–10:07 (SHOWED).
  - **CLARITY:** Clear.

- **RULE:** The primary profit objective for a 3:00 PM to 4:00 PM fade on a consolidation day is the exact 50% midpoint (equilibrium) of the day's high-to-low range.
  - **CATEGORY:** take profit
  - **NUMBERS:** 50% midpoint of the daily high and daily low.
  - **SOURCE:** 06:53–07:16, 09:09–09:16, 17:05–17:34 (SAID and SHOWED).
  - **CLARITY:** Clear.

- **RULE:** Secure partial profits upon reaching the 50% midpoint of the daily range, while allowing a runner position to target extreme liquidity (such as the daily sell-side low) or trailing the stop loss to protect open profits.
  - **CATEGORY:** trade management
  - **NUMBERS:** Partial exit taken on 4 of 6 contracts (66.7% partial), followed by a partial of 1 more contract (total 5 of 6 closed).
  - **SOURCE:** 09:10–09:17, 09:25–09:33, 18:31–18:40 (SAID and SHOWED).

## Conditions that Cancel a Setup / Filters (What NOT to do)

- **Do NOT trade trend-following or continuation breakout setups during the middle of the day on a consolidation day:** When an outside day fails to take an old low, the next day is prone to choppy whipsaw price action. Trading within the interior of the range yields random 50/50 probability and leads to account churn.
- **Do NOT chase upside breakouts above equal highs during the 3:00 PM to 4:00 PM window:** Retail traders view the late-session breakout as a continuation rally, but algorithmically it is an engineered liquidity run to fill institutional market-on-close orders before a sharp retracement back to equilibrium.
- **Do NOT enter short until lower timeframe confirmation occurs:** Do not short simply because price reached an old high; wait for an explicit swing low breakdown and the creation of a Fair Value Gap on the 1-minute chart.

## Ambiguities and Open Questions

- **Exact definition of "falling short" of an old low:** Michael notes that price fell short of the 3855 level by 1 handle (stopping at 3856), triggering the consolidation day profile. He does not specify the maximum distance (e.g., maximum points or ticks) that constitutes "falling short" before the day is considered an unfulfilled liquidity run versus an ordinary retracement.
- **Midpoint calculation basis:** ICT measures the midpoint using the absolute high of the day and low of the day. In real-time trading prior to 3:00 PM, the daily high was lower, and the final high was only established during the 3:00–4:00 PM sweep. Programmers must ensure the midpoint dynamically recalculates when the late-session sweep establishes a new high of the day.
